**Scope:** the 75 original videos in the supplied Yapping collection. **Purpose:** a grounded creative reference for Astra, the script-writing engine in AIOSCreator. **Completion:** 75/75 timestamped reviewed automatic transcripts, source analyses, readable reports and passing source QA. **Performance:** unknown for every source. This is a completed reference corpus, not evidence of winning ads or a trained/fine-tuned model.

## What the collection actually contains

The collection is speech-led UGC with several execution styles: direct address, live product use, voiceover over a routine, garment demonstrations, a two-person exchange, and contextual inserts. A single talking-head template would flatten useful differences between these references.

## The creative decision before the script

Choose one viewer tension and one concrete product action. Then choose a narrative pattern that can express both. A script should explain why this viewer would watch this product being used in this situation.

The script brief must contain:

1. The actual product and the approved facts that can be stated.
2. One audience tension or purchase hesitation.
3. One primary angle: the specific reason this product matters in this situation.
4. The footage or generation assets that can demonstrate the angle.
5. The creator format and permitted experience statements.
6. The market, language, destination and current offer from the campaign brief.
7. The desired duration and the meaningful differences between requested variants.

If a price, result, experience, dose, certification, endorsement or offer is missing, Astra must not fill it from a reference ad. Missing information becomes a brief question or is omitted from the script.

## Hook direction

The useful difference between hooks is the curiosity they create: a specific hesitation, an occasion, a tangible feature, a visible action, or a surprising format. Swapping the first sentence while repeating the same argument is weak batch variation.

Examples below are newly written directions, not transcripts or performance-tested hooks:

| Mechanism | Fresh direction | Product truth required |
|---|---|---|
|Specific objection|“Buying a strapless style? Check this construction detail first.”|The actual construction detail must exist and be visible.|
|Occasion|“A quick routine for when you want a lighter finish.”|The intended use and cosmetic wording must be supported.|
|Action-led curiosity|“Watch the step most product photos don't show.”|The footage must actually show a relevant step.|
|Format contrast|“Here is the same routine in a format you can carry.”|Portability and the comparison must be true.|
|Practical question|“What does this part actually do?”|An approved explanation and a clear close-up are needed.|

For a generated presenter, “I used this for three months,” “my trainer made me try it,” and “I lost 25 pounds” are not casual style choices. They assert personal history. Source 074 illustrates why a compelling opening and a transferable claim are different things.

Use the earliest practical visual action that supports the idea. Do not turn that direction into a universal three-second rule: this collection has no retention data.

## Script and visual direction must agree

Every scene has one job. Its dialogue, product action and text should support that job.

| Scene purpose | Spoken direction | Visual requirement |
|---|---|---|
|Make the concern specific|Name the real friction in plain language.|Show the relevant context or part.|
|Explain a feature|Use one approved fact at a time.|Point to, handle or operate the matching feature.|
|Demonstrate|Describe what the shot actually establishes.|Use the real product or approved reference asset.|
|Answer an objection|Address a practical hesitation proportionately.|Show the part/action that answers it where possible.|
|Close|Give one destination or next step.|Use the actual offer and destination supplied in the brief.|

Showing capsules does not prove their clinical effect. A body gesture does not prove weight loss. A face close-up does not establish a controlled before/after. A travel prop does not verify a trip. A visually pleasing final frame can support a demonstration without becoming a quantitative efficacy claim.

The strongest concrete demonstrations in this collection include breakfast preparation 002, garment construction 003/025/032, an awkward intermediate makeup stage 033, capsule mechanics 049, actual taste/opening friction 063, earplug components 067, and recipe preparation 068. What they prove is limited to the visible action and conditions documented in the source report.

## Natural delivery without invented history

Write speech that a creator can deliver comfortably: short thought units, concrete words, a reason for each gesture, a small pause during an action, and an ending suited to the chosen goal. A casual aside can make delivery less stiff when it follows the situation.

Do not fill every line with “literally,” “obsessed,” “game changer,” or exaggerated certainty. Those words appear in source advertising but are not the reason a demonstration works. Do not reproduce a distinctive source hook word for word as a new campaign.

Keep performance directions executable: point to the band, turn the jar label, open the packet, pause after tasting, look at the applied cheek. “Act extremely natural and viral” does not specify an action.

Source words-per-minute measurements are approximate. The collection median is 209.9 across whole clips, with ASR and timing limitations. That number describes the references; it is not a delivery target. Plan the actual dialogue against the available speaking time, then check it with the selected voice/delivery during production.

## Duration and editing

A useful planning equation is:

`planned words ≈ speaking seconds × chosen speaking rate /60`

Speaking seconds exclude deliberate pauses, silent demonstrations and the time needed to inspect an important visual. Use a project-approved speaking rate or a measured preview. If the draft cannot fit, remove a secondary argument before speeding up the voice.

A thirty-second adaptation might allocate 0–3s to the opening action, 3–8s to context, 8–20s to one explanation/demo, 20–26s to the main hesitation, and 26–30s to the next step. These are starter allocations, not findings about optimal retention. P03/P04 can spend more time on demonstration; P01 can reach the exception quickly; P10 may need a longer explanation.

Cut when a new argument, product action, useful angle or reaction enters. Continuous talking can be effective if the content develops. The visual-scene detector measures pixel changes, so it must not dictate a forced cut every two seconds.

The collection contains explicit link/cart prompts, recommendations, routine closes, questions invitations, and offers carried only by screen text. The current campaign objective decides the CTA.

A source's sale, stock warning, celebrity mention, gift, discount code or free shipping is historical source content. It does not establish today's offer or third-party endorsement. If no offer is supplied, do not invent one.

## Reference retrieval for AIOSCreator

Start with the requested UGC type. Within Yapping, select references based on the viewer tension, product group, demonstration assets, delivery format and target duration. Use market and language from the new brief; the source collection does not contain reliable market-performance metadata.

Retrieve two or three complementary evidence spans rather than every transcript. A practical bundle contains one narrative reference, one matching demonstration, and optionally one delivery reference. Record source IDs and approximate intervals in the draft rationale so a reviewer can trace the choices.

The bundled corpus has two distinct uses:

- **Creative reference:** abstract patterns, observed actions, delivery and editing observations.
- **Attributed source content:** transcript, source claims, old offers, names and uncertainties. These remain source-bound and cannot supply new product facts.

Original source records preserve their existing `runtime_eligible=false` and `editorial_approval=pending` fields. This delivery completes analysis and synthesis; it does not alter an application's runtime approval policy. The implementation guide explains how to use the abstract bible while keeping raw source content attributed.

## Batch variation

For three variants, change the central reason to watch:

1. An objection-first concept using the visible construction or mechanism.
2. An occasion-first concept showing use in the relevant setting.
3. An action-first concept opening on the tangible demonstration.

Each variant specifies its own primary angle, hook mechanism, product action, supporting argument and CTA. Preserve product identity and approved facts. Do not rotate the source creator's personal history into a generated host.

Track actual performance when campaigns are run. Until then, label variant choices as creative hypotheses. Later results should attach to the generated variant and its campaign, not retroactively mark a reference video as a winner.

## What Astra delivers

Return a concept rationale, source references with timestamps, timed scenes, spoken dialogue, on-screen text, specific actions, delivery notes, product-fact IDs and an edit/CTA plan. Keep the script executable for storyboard creation without turning every scene into a long model prompt.

Before handoff, check that the chosen angle is clear, important product facts have brief citations, actions are possible with supplied assets, dialogue fits the duration, captions agree with speech, and the CTA matches the campaign. Unknown facts remain unknown.
