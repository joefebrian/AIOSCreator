# Creator Campaign Scaling Playbook

## Why scale to creators
One person can only post so much content and reaches one account's distribution pattern. A creator campaign lets you test the same proven system across more faces, accounts, audiences, and executions.

The goal is not “more creators” by itself. The goal is more quality attempts around a mechanism that already has evidence.

## Do not launch too early
Before scaling, try to have:
- one clearly defined product angle
- one or more hooks that have shown signal
- a format creators can realistically reproduce
- product assets/access ready
- a clear CTA
- a way to measure performance

## Signals it is time to scale
Launch a creator campaign when one or more are true:
- one creative materially beats the account baseline
- the same angle works in multiple executions
- the product gets repeated positive curiosity in comments
- conversion/click data shows the angle has commercial value
- your own posting capacity is now the bottleneck
- you have enough budget to test multiple creators without judging the campaign from one post

## Phase 1 — Small creator test
Recommended structure:
- 5–15 creators
- 1–3 posts each
- 2–4 approved creative angles
- clear examples, but permission to make them native to each page
- fixed test window
- consistent tracking

Purpose: learn which creator profiles and executions transfer the winning mechanism.

## Phase 2 — Expand the winners
After the first wave:
- rank by useful performance, not just raw views
- identify repeated winning hooks/angles
- identify which creator archetypes work
- remove unclear or low-quality instructions
- turn top posts into new references
- recruit more creators similar to the winners

Increase volume around what worked instead of resetting the campaign every round.

## Phase 3 — Always-on system
Once repeatable:
- maintain a creator pipeline
- refresh references weekly or biweekly
- keep a live hook library
- push new winning posts back into the creative system
- rotate angles before fatigue becomes obvious
- keep part of the budget for exploration

Simple allocation model:
- 60–70% proven angles/formats
- 20–30% variations on proven ideas
- 10–20% new experiments

Adjust based on campaign maturity.

## What creators should receive
Every creator brief should contain:
- product explanation
- goal
- target audience
- required platform
- required CTA/link/tag
- 2–4 proven creative angles
- example references
- format options
- mandatory claims/rules
- prohibited claims/rules
- what must visually appear
- deadline
- compensation structure
- tracking/submission instructions

Avoid rigid word-for-word scripts unless consistency is legally or operationally necessary. Give creators the mechanism and guardrails, then let delivery match their page.

## Performance-based campaign option
If using a view/performance-based model, define clearly before creators post:
- RPM / CPM or other payout basis
- what counts as a verified/qualified view
- minimum views to qualify
- creator/post payout cap if any
- eligible platforms
- audience/geography requirements if any
- campaign budget
- end conditions
- content ownership/usage rights

Do not change payout rules retroactively.

## Scaling decision tree
If views are weak across creators → revisit hook/first frame/angle.

If views are strong but clicks are weak → product relevance or CTA may be weak.

If clicks are strong but conversions are weak → inspect offer/landing page/product fit before blaming creators.

If one creator wins but others do not → study creator fit and execution, not just the script.

If multiple creators win with the same angle → scale that angle aggressively while continuing controlled exploration.

## Campaign feedback loop
1. Creators post.
2. Capture the strongest posts.
3. Break down why they worked.
4. Add new winning hooks to the library.
5. Turn winners into new references.
6. Send the improved system into the next creator wave.
